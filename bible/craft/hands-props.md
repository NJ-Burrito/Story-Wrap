# Craft: hands, held props, writing (R-383 to R-392)

Source: the owner's notes F13 to F15 on Mix v4 (2026-10-23); cause analysis `kb/quality/rca-hands-writing.md`; checks `kb/quality/gate-spec.md` section L (`uv run tools/quality_gate.py hands <trace>`). Companion: `construction-catalogue.md` (hands are part of the catalogue: sheet 06 hands/grips), `biomechanics.md` (joint ranges).

## 1. How animators handle this (research, short)
Findings come from a search on 2026-10-23; hand-chart pages for 2D animation did not surface, so the 2D practice below is stated as a studio convention and marked as such.
- Hand rigs keep topology fixed: the same four fingers, thumb and joints in every pose; poses differ by joint angles, not by changing the structure. State-of-the-art survey of hand and finger modelling: https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf12595. Practical topology notes: https://blenderartists.org/t/hand-topology-proportions/490517 and the relaxed-pose and mid-range joint advice at https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2381949.
- Convention (studio practice, no single source): keep a hand chart per character (one drawing per grip and angle) and in-between a hand through its joint angles with an arc, never by cross-fading shapes or switching mode flags; a change that must be fast is hidden in a smear or a cut.
- Pen grip: the dynamic tripod (thumb, index and middle on the barrel, the pen resting in the web) is the standard target grip. Left-handers hold the pen slightly further from the point, with the index closer to the tip than the thumb, tilt the paper so its top-left corner is higher, and may hook the wrist over the line. Sources: https://www.hse.ie/babies-children/disabilities/handwriting/posture-pencil-grasp/, https://www.surreycc.gov.uk/schools-and-learning/schools/at-school/extra-support-for-your-child/helping-your-child-at-school/helping-left-handed-writers, https://www.growinghandsonkids.com/the-tripod-grasp.html.
- Arabic is written right to left, so the right-handed writer's hand travels leftwards along a line and ink grows from right to left; the nib then returns right and down for the next line; the strokes and dots of a word are added as the pen passes. This search returned no usable page for the stroke order; the statement is general knowledge of the script, and the check only needs the direction (`rtl`). A source with stroke order must be found before letter-level animation.
- Two-sided props (card, tag, page): a flat prop has a front and a back; which one the camera sees depends on its facing, and what is printed on each is fixed. A writer touches the face turned to her; the camera sees that face only if the card is turned to it. No animation text was found; this is first-principles geometry and is encoded as rules R-387 and R-388.

## 2. Rules

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| R-383 | **Topology.** Every hand has 4 fingers and a thumb in the order thumb, index, middle, ring, little; 3 phalanges per finger, 3 thumb bones; bone lengths constant. No grip, blend, smear or pose changes the count, order or lengths. |
| R-384 | **Continuity.** In one frame no finger joint moves more than 35 deg, the hand axis turns more than 40 deg, or the wrist travels more than 120 px (1080x1920). A faster change is a declared smear (1 to 2 frames) or sits on a cut. |
| R-385 | **Handedness from the bible.** The character bible names the writing hand and the giving hand. Hero writes with her LEFT hand and gives and takes with her RIGHT (`catalogue/characters/hero/character.json`, `handedness`). Grips are authored per hand (`-L`, `-R`). A grip for the other hand is used only mirrored on purpose (`mirrored: true`), including the wrist sign. The writing hand does the pen action; the other hand holds the paper. |
| R-386 | **Grip changes are in-betweened.** Changing grip, contact point (heel, palm, pinch, pen tip) or thumb mode takes at least 4 frames of a continuous blend (k from 0 to 1). Discrete flags (a `pinch` boolean, a contact string) are never switched mid-blend: drive them with a continuous weight (thumb target = lerp(rest, index pad, w)), and blend the contact offset with the same k. |
| R-387 | **Two-sided props.** A card, tag or page is modelled with a `front` and a `back` face, what is printed on each, and the face toward the camera in every frame (`facing`). Ink, text and print are drawn only on the face the camera sees; a face turning away is hidden, not drawn through. A thin edge (thickness) shows only while the card is turned edge-on, never as a back sheet. |
| R-388 | **Write on the face the nib touches.** The writer works on the face turned to her. If the viewer must watch it, the staging makes that the camera face: the card is held up toward the camera with the writing hand reaching round the near edge, the page lies on the counter facing up toward a high camera, or the camera is on the writer's side; a writer behind a prop never writes on its camera-facing face from the camera side. The staging is decided in the camera plan (F3) before drawing. |
| R-389 | **Writing is revealed under the nib.** Ink is a polyline in the paper's own coordinates, revealed by arc length; its leading end is the nib tip (within 6 px); it grows only while the nib is down and moving; it never shrinks, and it moves with the paper. Strokes appear in writing order, dots and diacritics after their letter body. |
| R-390 | **Direction of the script.** Arabic: the nib moves right to left along a line (leftwards on screen for a viewer who sees the written face), then returns to the right and down for the next line. English: left to right. The check measures the movement of the revealed end. |
| R-391 | **Pen contact.** While writing, the pen tip is on the paper surface (gap at most 6 px) and inside the paper; the pen leans 50 to 70 deg from the paper plane toward the writer's wrist; the nib lifts between strokes (gap grows, no ink). |
| R-392 | **Grip library per prop.** Each held prop has a catalogued grip with digit contact points (thumb, index, middle pad on the prop, in prop coordinates) and a layer order per camera angle, e.g. pen, front view, writing hand: paper, hand body, pen, index finger (the pen crosses under the index pad), thumb pad over the barrel. The renderer draws that order and exports it; the hand follows the prop's contact points, and the prop follows the hand through them. Paper is never above the hand that writes on it, except for the part the other hand pinches. |

## 3. Procedure for any new hand action (sketch stage, before animating)
1. Name the actor hand from the bible (R-385) and the prop with its faces (R-387).
2. Draw the grip sheet: one drawing per angle with digit contacts and the layer order (R-392); store it in the character catalogue (`hands/<grip>.json`: joint angles for 4 fingers and thumb, `grip_layers`, `contacts`).
3. Plan the action as key grips with in-between frames; mark smear or cut frames (R-384, R-386).
4. Staging check on the camera plan: which face of the prop faces the lens when the nib is down (R-388).
5. Build, export the trace fields listed in `rig-trace.schema.json`, run `quality_gate.py hands`.
