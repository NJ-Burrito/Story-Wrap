# Character design: from written profile to ink

Source: `research/05`, `research/01`. Templates: `../templates/artbook-character-spread.md`. Rules: R-046, R-070 to R-080. Characters must be fully written first (R-030); the drawing translates fixed facts and invents none.

## 1. The chain

Profile fact -> visible consequence -> silhouette and shape decision -> check against the profile.

- Job -> posture. Climate of origin -> clothing layers. Age -> hands, gait and wear, not only face lines.
- A character sheet exists to hold design, movement and personality constant across artists and time, and carries annotations of proportions, clothing, mannerisms, personality and speech [S https://blog.cg-wire.com/character-sheet-animation]. It cannot replace the written profile.

## 2. Shape language

Squares read stable, triangles sharp or dynamic, circles soft [S Binus DKV article via research/05]. It is a starting bias, not a verdict: an anxious square-built character is more interesting. One dominant shape family plus one contrasting; no two characters share a primary family (R-033, R-073).

## 3. Silhouette test

Fill the character solid black at three poses (idle, strongest action, reaction). A pose fails if its main gesture cannot be named. Hair, a prop or a costume element is the usual fix when two silhouettes are close; choose the element that carries biography. Disney Archives' Tony and Tina silhouette study is the best-known reference, but its text did not load [S https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/2020-05/T%26T_Silhouette-final2.pdf]. Check also at thumbnail size in black and white (R-072).

## 4. Exaggerating the extreme

Exaggerate one dimension at a time and only the one the bible names. Multiple extremes cancel each other's readability. The drawing amplifies a fact; it adds no gag.

## 5. Sheets (all ink only at this stage)

Turnaround (front, side, back, three-quarter, aligned on guidelines); expression sheet (neutral, the extreme trait, one reaction, plus head angles); pose sheet (idle, action, reaction, silhouettes); props sheet (scale, angles, moving parts, material notes); annotation sheet. The colour sheet is deferred until the style is decided (`../style-decision.md`).

## 6. Costume as biography

Three costume elements at most carry story, each traced to a bible fact (working rule [J]). Wear, repair and age tell history without explanation. Check every everyday object for connotations in its culture with the reviewer before drawing (Coco: footwear and implement changed on advice [S https://www.slashfilm.com/pixar-coco-set-visit/]).

## 7. Cultures, ages, bodies

Start from a person; the stereotype list is built from the bible and the reviewer; draw bodies of the stated age from life reference, not a youthful default; a body type is a fact of the person, not a joke, and not what the story is "about" unless the bible says so. Proportions per character: the common 7.5 to 8.5 head range is a starting point only; sources found no ethnic canons and show variation by individual, sex and age [S https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_proportions] (R-046).

## 8. Art-book format

No published book with exactly "gallery between catalogue and manga" was found. Closest forms: Takehiko Inoue's *Sumi* (full-page black-and-white ink plates, almost no text) [S https://parkablogs.com/content/book-review-sumi-vagabond-illustration-collection], Moebius's full-page ink plates (37 in the 1992 *Eloge de la Sieste*) [S Stuart N.G. Books listing], Mignola's heavy blacks held by a thin line [S https://hyperallergic.com/437231/mike-mignola-talk-hellboy-society-of-illustrators/]. Layouts are our design: `templates/artbook-character-spread.md`, `templates/artbook-world-spread.md`.

## 9. Pass criteria for a character spread

Hero plate silhouette-tested; turnaround aligned; three expressions; two to three props at scale; the trait sentence equals the bible's word for word; thumbnail B&W identification by a second agent succeeds (R-071, R-072, R-079).
