## 2. Cards (distilled; each rule is craft advice [J], not a measured result)

Episode note: `research/06` wrote its episode advice for 60 to 90 s. Our launch band is 30 to 60 s (R-111), so compress to one hook, one turn, one ending.

| Genre | Core promise | Must-have beats | Subversion hook | Episode open / close | Top failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Romance | Two people who should not work, pull until the ending earns it | Meeting with friction; forced proximity; near-intimacy then withdrawal; a threat (rival, past, secret); dark moment; return | Love interest with a reasonable fear; protagonist refuses | Open on contact; close on an irreversible choice or a reveal about each other | Obstacle one honest talk would fix |
| Thriller | Growing threat, no exit, a reveal that recasts the past | Threat aimed at protagonist; failed escape; ticking constraint; trusted betrayal; larger-than-believed reversal | Expert caused the danger | Open on threat in motion; close when the threat changes shape; show the clock every episode | Deadline resets with no cost |
| Fantasy | A world bigger than the page with a price for power | Arrival/awakening; mentor who withholds; trial that shows rules; cost paid; final fight using taught rules | Unwilling chosen one; prophecy wrong about the enemy | Open on an unseen magic action; close on a rule broken or a cost | Lore dump; power with no price |
| Action/adventure | Physical stakes, escalating set pieces | Opening that shows competence; objective; escalating obstacles; outmatched improvisation; payoff climax | Strength with a cost; sidekick's skill wins | Open mid-motion; close in an inescapable position | Fights that change nothing; unreadable space |
| Mystery | A fair puzzle | Discovery; first interview; red herring; complication; reconstruction; confrontation (Story Grid names discovery and confrontation) | Detective wrong for emotional reasons | Open on the crime or a wrong detail; close on a clue that contradicts belief | Clues only in the final scene |
| Horror | Dread that builds, then an unexpected fear | Disturbed ordinary world; partly seen threat; disbelief; rule learned at a cost; escalation; changed protagonist | Sceptic is right and the threat ordinary | Open on a detail out of place; close on the threat's shape or a first step toward it | Monster explained too early |
| Sci-fi | One new idea and what it does to people | World-changing device; first reaction; demonstration; cost; rule turned on protagonist; choice | n/a in source | Open on the new thing in an ordinary moment; close on its first cost | n/a |
| Slice of life | Ordinary people, an unexpected truth | Routine; disturbance; small problem; changed relationship; quiet recognition; altered routine | Kishotenketsu: no central conflict, a twist that reconciles | Open on a specific routine; close on a small visible change | Nothing at stake |
| Comedy | A situation that escalates because of the character | Clear want; plan; failure; reversal; flaw exposed; payoff twist | Humour from character, not situation | Open on a confident plan; close on a new failure | Joke on a major payoff |
| Drama | A human choice under pressure | Wound; pressure relationship; decision; unavoidable consequence; reckoning | n/a | Open on a line revealing a wound; close on a sentence that cannot be taken back | n/a |

Frameworks behind the cards: Story Grid conventions (core value, conventions, obligatory moments), Save the Cat's ten story types, Truby's *Anatomy of Genres*, kishotenketsu [S research/06]. Story Grid per-genre lists and Truby's contents were not retrieved in full (Q-11).

## 3. Overlap points to use in blends

- Romance and thriller share the betrayal: use it as the romance's dark moment.
- Fantasy and action share the set piece: give it a world rule.
- Mystery and horror share the wrongness and the withheld rule: use the clue as the first sign.
- Kishotenketsu carries a slice-of-life episode without breaking the act shape. [S research/06]

Method for combining 7 or 8: `genre-blending.md`.
