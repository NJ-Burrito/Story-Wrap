# Worldbuilding

Source: `research/07` part 2. Template: `../templates/world-bible.md`. Rules: R-041, R-060 to R-062.

## 1. Build from the story outward

Minimum bible: where the characters are, what they can do, what they cannot, what the place costs them. Everything else is added when a scene asks. A fact that never reaches a scene is decoration; that is allowed only as a marked choice (R-062) [S https://dabblewriter.com/articles/worldbuilding-bible].

## 2. Sanderson's laws as constraints [S, author guidelines not rules]

- First: the power to solve conflict with magic is proportional to how well the reader understands it. Choose hard (clear rules) or soft (deliberately vague) on purpose [S https://brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-first-law/].
- Second: limitations beat powers; the best limit says something about the power's nature [S https://www.brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-second-law/].
- Third: expand what you have before adding new. Trace one change through politics, economy and daily life; let different cultures answer one shared power instead of inventing one per culture [S https://brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-third-law-of-magic/]. This is the multi-world rule (R-060).

## 3. Per-world fields (what to write, in order)

1. Geography: location, neighbours and links (trade, war, distance); climate and resources; travel cost in days, money or danger; the one feature a character notices first. A geography that creates no choice is not yet useful.
2. History: key figures and events on one shared timeline plus a per-world timeline; mark what each main character remembers and what they were told.
3. Culture: customs and rituals, hierarchy, art, and a naming rule (sound pattern, titles, whether names carry meaning). A full language is optional.
4. Economy: currency, trades and their geographic cause, who holds wealth. Economy is where worlds stop feeling like costumes.
5. Belief: only what shows in daily life (before a journey, mourning, what is never said aloud).
6. Power (magic or technology): shared series rule, soft or hard and why, limits and costs, daily-life ripple.
7. Respect and sourcing: invented or based on a named real culture; sources; reviewer from that culture (name, date) or "pending" (R-041).

## 4. Several worlds in one series

- Series page: spine, tonal range, shared power rule, shared timeline, heart.
- One page per world: the fields above plus flavour genre, tonal register, story function (why this world is in the series).
- Links both ways: characters who live there; timeline events that happen there.
- Shared across worlds: the power's core rule, basic physics and biology, currency or calendar if worlds trade. Different per world: culture, economy, belief, flavour genre, register.

## 5. Real cultures

No authoritative style guide exists [S research/07]. What the sources agree on: research the culture, not the stereotype; bring the reviewer in during worldbuilding, not at the end; do not lift real-world tropes (for example evil peoples standing in for Indigenous peoples); consult people from the culture [S https://britishfantasysociety.org/how-authenticity-reading-can-benefit-fantasy-writers/ , https://www.enworld.org/posts/7813978/]. Avatar is the most-cited positive case yet its team was largely white, so intent does not replace review.

Working rule (R-041): every culture is either invented from its own rules or researched from named sources with a native reviewer before the bible closes.

Production precedents for early advisers: Coco brought advisers in earlier than any prior Pixar film and changed a character's footwear and implement on their advice; Moana's Oceanic Trust shaped Maui's design (broad frame, thick hair) [S research/05 via ScreenDaily, Slashfilm, NBC News].

## 6. Checks before a world is "closed"

Rules testable? Costs, limits and daily-life effect for every power? One shared rule across worlds? Every culture sourced and reviewed? Every fact either used in a scene or tagged decoration?
