# Character writing

Source: `research/08`, `research/05`. Templates: `../templates/character-bible.md`, `../templates/ensemble-contrast-matrix.md`. Rules: R-030 to R-046. No length limit per character; completeness is checked by field, not by page count.

## 1. Order of writing (do not reorder)

1. Ghost -> Lie -> Want -> Need -> Cost (the inner engine).
2. Values and contradictions.
3. Extreme trait and the habit that shows it.
4. Voice card, tells, habits, skills, interests.
5. Origin and culture (shapes the form of the want, not the want).
6. Appearance and movement (follows from 1 to 5, never the reverse).
7. Relationship web.
8. Contrast matrix against the rest of the cast.

Personality built from a visual idea is the usual cause of stock characters [J research/08].

## 2. The inner engine

- **Ghost** (Truby's term): one dated past event with one witness and one lasting consequence. Mundane events qualify. Mark the rest of the life history "not for the screen" unless needed [S https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/guide-to-backstory/ ; J on the format].
- **Lie**: a false belief the ghost installed, stated as a sentence a person could say. A mood is not a lie.
- **Want**: the external goal chased to fix an inner gap. **Need**: the truth that answers the lie; it may require giving up the want [S https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/character-arcs-3/].
- **Cost**: what pursuing the want costs, shown somewhere.

| Link | Question | Failure sign |
|---|---|---|
| Ghost | What one event taught this lie? | "Had a hard childhood" |
| Lie | What belief did it install? | A mood |
| Want | What outer thing do they chase because of the lie? | No link to the lie |
| Need | What truth ends the lie? | Just the opposite of the want |
| Cost | What does the want cost? | Never shown |

Arc types: positive (overcomes the lie), flat (holds the truth, tested), negative (falls to the lie). The cast needs all three (R-034).

## 3. Extreme and human [J research/08]

1. Extreme about one thing, ordinary about everything else.
2. The trait wins something and loses something in every scene where it shows.
3. The trait is right sometimes.
4. Explained by the ghost, not a label; the audience can guess the reason before it is stated.
5. Another character knows the trait and reacts to it.
6. Show it in a boring scene (queue, meal, waiting room), not only in crises.
7. Show it in a habit, not a speech: "re-stacks the cups after every guest leaves, even while the guest is still there".

Write three contradictions: belief vs behaviour, two loyalties, self-image vs how others see them. Each needs a cause in the ghost. Separate what they say they value, what they do under pressure, what they refuse to do.

## 4. Voice card

Register (and one register never used); sentence shape; vocabulary field from their life; address habits; what they refuse to say (linked to the lie); languages: spoken at home, when angry, with family; change under stress; three test lines written without the character's name. Mixed-language lines are checked by a native speaker (R-044).

## 5. Habits, skills, tells, interests

Two interests (one feeds the plot, one private) and one thing they are bad at. Each skill: where learned and what it costs. A daily habit with time and place. One tell (lying, afraid, in love) not used by anyone else in the cast, written as one drawable line. Habits are the cheapest characterisation in animation.

## 6. Psychology models (question lists only)

- Big Five: a checklist and brainstorming aid; broad labels flatten; scores depend on context; no motive or plot; cross-cultural stability disputed (a 2024 South Indian study questioned the factor structure) [S https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10725210]. Do not use as a cultural map.
- Enneagram: a 2021 systematic review of 104 samples found mixed evidence and did not recover nine types [S https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33332604/]. Use for motivation vocabulary only.
- Attachment: critics note an American-sample origin and reliance on recalled childhood [S research/08]. Write it as a scene behaviour ("reads silence as withdrawal and sends three messages").

## 7. Relationship web

Per main character: the five closest people, the relationship in one word, the secret each holds, the debt each owes; A-to-B and B-to-A written separately; at least one relationship that tests the lie and one that offers the need. A table, not a diagram.

## 8. Writing across cultures

Eight working rules, synthesised and to be checked by readers in each culture [S Writing the Other (Shawl and Ward), Jericho Writers, MasterClass; J]: build from a person; research the everyday not the exotic; use named sources per culture; give each character a contradiction unrelated to culture; consult paid readers per culture and language before sketches are approved and record what they changed; native check for dialogue; no tokenism; culture is never the punchline. A reader is one input, not proof of authenticity (critics argue readers can serve as publisher risk management [S https://spectator.com/article/the-rise-of-the-sensitivity-reader/]). One reader cannot represent a community.

Method precedents: Coco and Moana advisers (see `worldbuilding.md` section 5).

## 9. Ensemble contrast

Every pair differs on at least 3 of 10 axes; same value on 2 or more of the first 4 axes (lie, want, ghost type, coping habit) means rewrite. Matrix template: `../templates/ensemble-contrast-matrix.md`. Run it before any drawing (R-032).
