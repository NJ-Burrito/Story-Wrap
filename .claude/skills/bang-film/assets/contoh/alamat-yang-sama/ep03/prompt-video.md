```
VIDEO STYLE & FORMAT
30-second scene in the style of a modern Indonesian short drama. ONE continuous reunion —
a son and his mother in one room, NOT a trailer, NOT a montage — covered like a real film:
cut between MULTIPLE camera positions, angles changing every few seconds while the moment
flows continuously. Slow pacing with long pauses; nothing is explained. Naturalistic,
underplayed acting — no crying, no embracing, no swelling emotion. Look: warm-neutral
filmic colour grading, fine film grain, shallow depth of field, accurate Indonesian skin
tones. This is a quiet family drama, NOT horror — the mother is not frightening, she is
simply there. NOT sinetron, NO dramatic zooms, NO melodrama, dialogue never rushed.

LANGUAGE
All spoken dialogue in INDONESIAN (Bahasa Indonesia), Jakarta register, casual spoken
form — not formal news-reader Indonesian. Speak the lines exactly as written in the
breakdown below: do not translate them, do not paraphrase them, do not invent extra
lines. The mother refers to herself as "Ibu", as Indonesian mothers do. No subtitles, no
captions, no on-screen text of any kind burned into the frame. Character names are never
spoken aloud unless they appear inside the quotation marks.

OPTICS
Base 35mm spherical at T2.0 — natural perspective, SHALLOW depth of field. Close-ups step
up to 85mm (FOV approx 24 degrees) for face isolation; the final medium close-up uses
50mm. The opening detail on the photograph uses a 100mm macro. Per-shot lens is specified
in the breakdown. No wide-angle distortion, no fisheye, no zoom moves.

CAMERA
Every cut moves to a DIFFERENT physical position — in front of the cabinet at the height
of the photograph, in the kitchen doorway, beside the cabinet, low beside the stack of
boxes, behind the floral sofa, and low beside the white plastic chair. Never two
consecutive shots from the same position. One very slow push in the final shot;
everything else locked off. No handheld shake, no orbit, no crane. No slow-motion.

LIGHTING
One large soft source: midday daylight coming through the frosted-glass sliding door,
milky and directionless near the door, falling off quickly toward the back of the room.
No lamps or ceiling lights on. Faces turned toward the door glow softly; faces turned
away go into deep warm-grey shadow. The only warm note is the brown cardboard of the
stacked boxes catching the door light. Low-key, gentle contrast, never flat front light.
The glass of the framed photograph catches a soft reflection of the door.

CRITICAL
TWO characters only, nobody else. Both keep the exact face, hairstyle and clothing from
their reference images in every shot. Both have a small dark mole on the LEFT cheekbone —
keep it on both of them, in the same place, in every shot where their faces are seen. No
face swapping, no morphing, no duplicated faces, no merging of the two faces. Accurate
Indonesian lip-sync — only the speaking character's mouth moves; the other stays still
and listens.

CHARACTERS (from reference images)
ADIT [gambar_adit] — 27, faded green driver jacket with no logos, grey t-shirt, dark
jeans, black phone pouch across the chest, facing his mother for the first time in
years.
IBU [gambar_ibu] — 63, faded brown batik house dress, beige cardigan, grey hair clipped
back, wooden cane, standing in the bedroom doorway, then sitting in the white plastic
chair by the door.

LOCATION (from references)
The living room [gambar_ruang_tengah] — Small living room of the same house, seen from
inside. The frosted-glass sliding door fills one wall, daylight coming through it soft
and milky. A white plastic chair placed close to the door, facing it. An old
floral-patterned sofa pushed against the side wall, with an open doorway to the kitchen
in the same wall. A doorway to a back bedroom hung with a faded floral curtain. Pale
ceramic floor tiles. A low dark wooden cabinet with a single small framed photograph on
top. Against the far wall, a waist-high stack of small brown cardboard boxes, every one
still sealed with clear tape, none opened.
The boxes in the stack — small brown cardboard box, roughly shoebox size, wrapped in
clear packing tape, no labels, no printed text, no logos
The photograph on the cabinet — small framed photograph in a thin dark wooden frame: a
boy about ten years old in a white short-sleeved school shirt, a small dark mole on his
left cheekbone, smiling, faded colours

AUDIO
Near silence. A clock ticking somewhere in the house, never seen. The single tap of a
wooden cane on ceramic tile. The plastic chair creaking as she sits. Clear tape tearing
in the last seconds. NO musical score until the final three seconds, then one soft
sustained note on an acoustic guitar. Dialogue quiet and unhurried, with real silence
between lines.

MOVEMENT NOTE
Ibu and Adit run on OPPOSITE rhythms. Ibu barely moves — her body is still, both hands on
the cane, and only her eyes follow him; when she speaks she does not lean or gesture.
Adit is the one who moves, slowly and with effort, as if each step costs him something;
he avoids her eyes when he asks his question and looks at the boxes instead. They are
never mirrored. When he finally sits on the floor beside her chair, it is the first time
he sits in the whole story — it must read as giving in, not resting.

SHOT BREAKDOWN

SCENE [00:00-00:30] RUANG TENGAH — one continuous reunion, cut between positions:

SHOT 1 (0-3s), 100mm macro EXTREME CLOSE-UP, static, in front of the cabinet at the
height of the photograph: Adit's thumb wipes a line of dust from the glass of the small
framed photograph. Off-screen, the single tap of a wooden cane on ceramic tile. His thumb
stops.

SHOT 2 (3-8s), 35mm WIDE, static, from the kitchen doorway: ADIT turns from the cabinet.
Across the room, in the bedroom doorway with the faded floral curtain pushed aside, IBU
stands — one hand on the door frame, the other on her cane. Neither of them moves. No
dialogue — five seconds of the clock.

SHOT 3 (8-12s), 85mm CLOSE-UP on IBU, static, from beside the cabinet: soft door light on
her face, the small dark mole on her left cheekbone. She speaks quietly, as if continuing
a conversation that never stopped.

IBU: "Kamu masih nggak pernah duduk, ya."

SHOT 4 (12-15s), 85mm CLOSE-UP on ADIT, static, low from beside the stack of boxes: the
same small dark mole on his left cheekbone. His mouth opens; barely any sound comes out.

ADIT (almost no voice): "Bu..."

SHOT 5 (15-22s), 35mm MEDIUM two-shot, static, from behind the floral sofa: Adit crosses
the room, takes her elbow, and walks her slowly to the white plastic chair by the frosted
door. She sits; the chair creaks. He does not look at her — he looks at the stack of
boxes.

ADIT: "Kenapa nggak ada yang dibuka?"

IBU (flat, no performed sadness): "Isinya bukan yang Ibu tunggu."

SHOT 6 (22-30s), 50mm MEDIUM CLOSE-UP, very slow push in, low beside the white plastic
chair: Adit lowers himself and sits on the floor beside her chair. He pulls the top box
from the stack, rests it on his knees, and begins to tear the clear tape. Her hand comes
to rest on his shoulder. No dialogue — the tape tearing, then the single guitar note.

The shot ends mid-tear, the box still closed — no freeze frame, no fade to black.
```
